The gap

Screening is built around the building, not the person.

Every established provider works the same way. The person travels to a centre, gives blood, goes home, and waits for a result to arrive days later, usually by post or portal. It works. It is also the single biggest reason most people never get screened at all.

Take the assessment to where people already are and the participation problem disappears. That is the whole idea, and it is only possible if you remove the two things that tie screening to a building: the laboratory, and the clinician.

Centre-based screening

  • The person travels to a fixed location
  • Venous blood draw, sent to a laboratory
  • Results returned in days, remotely
  • Requires a phlebotomist or nurse
  • Sold per person, in packages
  • Participation limited by willingness to travel

HeartsSight

  • The assessment travels to the workforce
  • Point-of-care measurement, nothing leaves site
  • Results discussed in the room, same visit
  • Delivered by a trained technician
  • Sold per head, by the day, to organisations
  • Participation limited only by the diary
The assessment

What actually happens in the forty minutes.

A single seated session. Nine instruments, one operator, one integrated result. The person is measured, not sampled, and the reading is complete before they stand up.

Cardiovascular

Four-limb blood pressure, heart rhythm and heart rate variability, digital pulse wave analysis for arterial elasticity.

Autonomic and stress

Heart rate variability seated and standing, orthostatic response, tension index. The measured physiology of the stress response.

Body composition

Bio-impedance for fat and lean mass, fluid distribution, phase angle and cellular integrity, waist and hip ratio.

Metabolic

Capillary blood glucose, urine chemistry, hydration and specific gravity, saliva pH.

Respiratory

Spirometry and blood oxygen saturation.

Thermal and inflammatory

Multi-site infrared temperature including thyroid differential.

Evidence

We would rather tell you exactly what the research shows.

Most of this sector quotes a single flattering percentage and hopes nobody asks. The method behind HeartsSight was evaluated in controlled, blinded, ethically approved studies, and we would rather publish what those studies actually found, including where they found nothing.

Every figure below is traceable to a primary document, and the ethics approval exists in certified translation, so it can be checked rather than taken on trust.

What the studies support, and what they do not.

Well supported

  • Cardiovascular assessment. The strongest and most consistent result across both cohorts.
  • Body composition and obesity.
  • Thyroid and genitourinary indicators.
  • Test-retest reproducibility. 47 patients reassessed by different operators, four to sixty-four days apart.
  • The psychophysiological stress model. Eleven measured factors accounted for 72% of variance in perceived stress on a validated instrument.

Not supported by our own data

  • Respiratory conditions. The original instrument set showed no useful signal. Spirometry was added specifically in response.
  • Diabetes and metabolic disease. Weak in both studies. A glucose meter was added specifically in response.
  • Liver and gallbladder conditions.
  • Musculoskeletal and structural conditions. Nothing in the assessment measures the spine or the gut lining, and the results reflect that.
  • Any claim to replace a diagnostic pathway. This is an assessment that tells you where to look.
The two instruments named above were added because our own studies exposed those weaknesses. We think a development record is worth more than a headline number, and we would rather be the company that publishes both.

Ethics approval to international standard

Sealed

A formal data lock before unblinding

The analysis was time-stamped and escrowed with an independent key holder before any medical diagnosis was released. The analytical decisions were therefore fixed before anyone could see whether they produced a favourable answer.

Operators were contractually barred from being told any known diagnosis, and confirmatory tests were withheld until our result had been returned. Neither side could see the other's answer.

The original science

Stress is not a soft measure. We measure it.

The most distinctive finding in eighteen years of work is also the one nobody else is looking at. Independent statistical analysis found that eleven measured physiological factors accounted for 72% of the variance in participants' perceived stress scores, on the validated Perceived Stress Scale, with significant relationships to cardiac ejection time, very low frequency power, waist and hip dimensions, heart rate and blood pressure.

Not a questionnaire. Not a wellbeing score. The measurable physiology of allostatic load, taken in the same forty minutes as everything else, in a working population of firefighters, lifeguards and nurses.

That section of the independent report was added between drafts. The reviewers arrived at it from the data rather than being handed it as a premise, which is the strongest form a finding can take.

Eighteen years

Not a product that appeared last year.

This began in a general practice, went through two hospital systems on two continents, and has been refined continuously since. The instrument set today is deliberately not the instrument set we started with.

2004 to 2008

Method developed in general practice in London and the north of England. Written standard operating procedures and a certification programme established, one procedure per test.

2009 to 2010

Prospective blinded study at the Heart Institute, Hadassah Hebrew University Medical Center, Jerusalem. Delivered by two senior nurses. Assessment duration measured at forty minutes.

2010 to 2012

Two controlled studies in St Petersburg across 467 patients, under ethics approval to Declaration of Helsinki and ICH GCP standards, with independent clinical review and a formal data lock.

2012 onward

Three tests withdrawn on the evidence. Spirometry, oximetry, blood glucose and refractometry added, two of them in direct response to the weaknesses our own studies identified.

Today

A portable assessment, a hosted analysis platform, a four-document reporting set and a certification programme for the people who deliver it.
