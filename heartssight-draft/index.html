The problem being solved
£212bnThe annual cost to the UK of economic inactivity caused by ill health. Seven per cent of GDP, and close to seventy per cent of all income tax collected.
Keep Britain Working, final report, March 2026

The country is not short of treatment. It is short of early detection.

2.8 million people are out of work through long-term sickness. Once someone leaves the workforce for health reasons, only 3.8% return within four years. The economics of that are brutal and they are almost entirely decided before anyone becomes a patient.

Screening is the obvious answer and it has an obvious problem: the people most likely to need it are the least likely to take a morning off, travel to a clinic and give blood. Coverage stays low precisely where risk is highest.

The gap

Screening is built around the building, not the person.

Every established provider works the same way. The person travels to a centre, gives blood, goes home, and waits for a result to arrive days later, usually by post or portal. It works. It is also the single biggest reason most people never get screened at all.

Take the assessment to where people already are and the participation problem disappears. That is the whole idea, and it is only possible if you remove the two things that tie screening to a building: the laboratory, and the clinician.

The assessment

What actually happens in the forty minutes.

A single seated session. Nine instruments, one operator, one integrated result. The person is measured, not sampled, and the reading is complete before they stand up.

Cardiovascular

Four-limb blood pressure, heart rhythm and heart rate variability, digital pulse wave analysis for arterial elasticity.

Autonomic and stress

Heart rate variability seated and standing, orthostatic response, tension index. The measured physiology of the stress response.

Body composition

Bio-impedance for fat and lean mass, fluid distribution, phase angle and cellular integrity, waist and hip ratio.

Metabolic

Capillary blood glucose, urine chemistry, hydration and specific gravity, saliva pH.

Respiratory

Spirometry and blood oxygen saturation.

Thermal and inflammatory

Multi-site infrared temperature including thyroid differential.

Evidence

We would rather tell you exactly what the research shows.

Most of this sector quotes a single flattering percentage and hopes nobody asks. The method behind HeartsSight was evaluated in controlled, blinded, ethically approved studies, and we would rather publish what those studies actually found, including where they found nothing.

Every figure below is traceable to a primary document, and the ethics approval exists in certified translation, so it can be checked rather than taken on trust.

What the studies support, and what they do not.

Well supported

  • Cardiovascular assessment. The strongest and most consistent result across both cohorts.
  • Body composition and obesity.
  • Thyroid and genitourinary indicators.
  • Test-retest reproducibility. 47 patients reassessed by different operators, four to sixty-four days apart.
  • The psychophysiological stress model. Eleven measured factors accounted for 72% of variance in perceived stress on a validated instrument.

Not supported by our own data

  • Respiratory conditions. The original instrument set showed no useful signal. Spirometry was added specifically in response.
  • Diabetes and metabolic disease. Weak in both studies. A glucose meter was added specifically in response.
  • Liver and gallbladder conditions.
  • Musculoskeletal and structural conditions. Nothing in the assessment measures the spine or the gut lining, and the results reflect that.
  • Any claim to replace a diagnostic pathway. This is an assessment that tells you where to look.
The two instruments named above were added because our own studies exposed those weaknesses. We think a development record is worth more than a headline number, and we would rather be the company that publishes both.

The original science

Stress is not a soft measure. We measure it.

The most distinctive finding in eighteen years of work is also the one nobody else is looking at. Independent statistical analysis found that eleven measured physiological factors accounted for 72% of the variance in participants' perceived stress scores, on the validated Perceived Stress Scale, with significant relationships to cardiac ejection time, very low frequency power, waist and hip dimensions, heart rate and blood pressure.

Not a questionnaire. Not a wellbeing score. The measurable physiology of allostatic load, taken in the same forty minutes as everything else, in a working population of firefighters, lifeguards and nurses.

That section of the independent report was added between drafts. The reviewers arrived at it from the data rather than being handed it as a premise, which is the strongest form a finding can take.

The hard questions

The things a serious buyer asks in the first ten minutes.

Answered here rather than in a meeting, because a buyer who has to extract these one at a time will assume the answers are worse than they are.

Is this a medical device, and is it regulated?

The assessment is delivered as a health assessment service, not as a diagnostic device, and every component instrument is an established, commercially available medical device used within its own intended purpose. The regulatory characterisation of the combined analysis is under formal review with an independent regulatory consultancy, and we will publish the outcome rather than describe it ourselves.

What we will not do is claim a status we have not established. If that answer is less convenient than a badge, it is also the only one worth giving.

Why does the assessment not require a clinician on site?

Because the measurements are physiological and standardised, and because the interpretation is done by the platform against a defined framework rather than by clinical judgement in the room. The operator takes readings; the system produces the report; the practitioner edition tells whoever holds clinical responsibility what to check and when to refer.

This is not theoretical. The method has been delivered twice inside other people's health systems by their own staff: three certified staff at a Russian federal medical centre, and two senior nurses at a Jerusalem teaching hospital after two weeks of training.

What happens when the assessment finds something serious?

Findings are flagged in two tiers: for discussion with a practitioner, or for onward referral. The practitioner edition carries specific guidance for each flag, including whether the person is already under medical care and what to check before referring. The individual receives their own report, in plain language, with the next step stated.

The assessment is designed to route people, not to conclude for them.

You publish your weak results. Why?

Because they are findable. The studies are documented, the reports exist, and anyone conducting proper diligence will locate them. A company that has published its own null results has nothing left to be discovered.

It is also more useful. Knowing that respiratory detection was poor with the original instrument set, and that spirometry was added specifically in response, tells you considerably more about how this has been developed than a single headline percentage ever could.

What about the higher percentages published previously?

Earlier material quoted figures of 96.5% and 96.6%. Both are real numbers, and both describe something narrower than the phrasing suggested. The first is cumulative agreement reached across two years, including cases reclassified after later confirmation. The second is agreement against a composite clinical burden score rather than against angiography alone, where the figure is 77.4%.

We have reviewed every published claim against its source document and restated the ones that did not survive. The figures on this page are the ones that do.

How is it priced?

Per head, by the day, with a minimum volume. The equipment is leased rather than sold, so there is no capital purchase before you have seen it work, and the platform is charged per active unit. Training and certification are priced per operator.

For occupational health providers the commercial shape is white-label capacity: your staff, your client relationships, our method and reporting.

What happens to the data?

Nothing physical leaves the site. Readings are captured on site and processed on a hosted platform. Individuals consent explicitly, including a separate consent for anonymised research use, and the employer receives an aggregate picture that does not identify anyone.

How large is the evidence base, honestly?

Two controlled studies covering 467 patients between 2010 and 2012, one 35-patient cardiac study against angiography in 2009, and one 70-patient outcome pilot in 2004. Ethically approved, blinded, with independent clinical review and a formal data lock.

It is a solid foundation and it is not a modern regulatory dossier. Sensitivity and specificity were specified in the protocols and have not yet been published, which is the honest gap. The work to close it is under way and we would rather say so than imply it is already done.
